Comma

User manual · version 1.0 · Windows, VST3 and standalone

1. Welcome

Comma is a synthesizer built around one idea: every note you play is a chord of up to eight voices. Each voice plays the note at its own ratio (1 = the note, 2 = an octave up, 1.5 = a fifth...) and each voice has its own engine: additive partials, a struck object, a chaotic system, logic gates, wavetables, grains, noise. Around the voices sit filters and EQs, a rail of effects you can reorder, and a modulation system with 32 sources, a 32-link matrix, eight macros and an XY pad.

Comma is monophonic: it plays one note at a time (the last key held wins). The chord comes from the eight voices and their ratios, not from playing several keys. This is what keeps the eight engines affordable and the chord always in tune with itself.

Why "Comma"

In music theory a comma is a tiny interval: the gap that is always left over when pure intervals are stacked. Twelve pure fifths overshoot seven octaves by the Pythagorean comma (about 23.5 cents); four pure fifths overshoot a pure major third by the syntonic comma (81/80, about 21.5 cents). Equal temperament hides these gaps by spreading them over the twelve semitones. Comma works on the other side: its ratios can be pure (the gold die of the ratio presets rolls chords in just intervals), its dissonance circuit can lay the partials on a grid of 12 to 31 notes per octave, and a few cents of detune between voices are enough to make a chord beat.

Hover any control to see what it does: the ? button at the top turns these hints on and off. Every control in this manual has its hint in the plugin.

2. Installation and license

What the installer does

  • Comma.vst3 goes into the common VST3 folder (C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3), where every host looks.
  • Comma.exe, the standalone application, goes into Program Files\HavFun Company\Comma, with a Start menu shortcut (a desktop shortcut is optional).
  • The factory bank (presets, wavetables, samples, filter and EQ curves...) goes into Documents\Comma. The installer never overwrites a file that is already there and never removes this folder when you uninstall: from then on, these are your sounds.

Comma runs on 64-bit Windows. Formats: VST3 and standalone. The plugin is an instrument: it needs MIDI from the host. It also has an optional stereo Sidechain input, used by the Sidechain effect (Input mode) and by the Follow mod source; turn it on in your host's routing.

Trial and activation

Comma runs fully for 14 days from the first launch. When the trial ends the sound fades out and the license window covers the interface until you activate. Reinstalling does not restart the trial.

  1. Buy Comma: Menu → License... → Buy Comma opens the store.
  2. You receive a license key by email. Paste it into the license window and press Activate. An internet connection is needed once.
  3. A key works on 3 computers. Deactivate frees this computer, to use the key on another one.

After activation Comma works offline. It checks the key with the store now and then; being offline changes nothing. Only the key and an anonymous fingerprint of the computer are sent, nothing else. The license window shows the state: days left in the trial, or "Activated on this computer" with the key partly masked.

3. Quick start

  1. Load Comma on an instrument track (or open the standalone) and play a note: you hear the startup preset, DeFaUlT.
  2. Click the arrows of the preset list at the top, or the gold die beside it, to hear other presets.
  3. Open the Synthesis tab: each row is a voice. Change a voice's Ratio and the chord changes. Click the engine box (Add, Wave...) to choose what the voice is.
  4. Click a voice's number to mute it, its headphones to hear it alone.
  5. Open FX, switch an effect on with the icon square at the left of its rail button, and drag the button up or down to change the order of the effects.
  6. Drag a Mod 1 button (bottom right) onto any knob: that knob now moves with Mod 1. Hover the Mod button to see everything it moves.
  7. Save your sound: open the preset list menu and choose Save As, or use the Presets tab.

4. The window

  1. Main tabs: Synthesis (the eight voices: their engines and their pages), EQ's/Filters (EQ A-D, F1-F4, Modal, Warmth), Modulation (the mod sources and the matrix), FX (the effects rail), Presets (the preset browser).
  2. Voice pages (Synthesis tab): Engine, Attack, PM, Diss, Warp, Mangle, Pluck, Motion, Spectron, Capture, Env, Punch. They follow the signal, left to right. The icon before a page's name lights when that page is doing something on at least one voice.
  3. Scale: snaps the notes you play to a scale (see Playing).
  4. ?: turns the hints on or off.
  5. The preset list, its arrows and its two dice.
  6. The ratio presets: chords for the eight voices, with their own dice. Beside them, Mutate and its strength (see The dice).
  7. Menu: colour theme, window size, license; in the standalone application, its tempo.
  8. The voice heads: ratio, detune, pan, engine, mute, solo, dice and a live spectrum for each voice. The column scrolls to V8.
  9. The voice body: the page chosen in 2, for each voice.
  10. The Global / Sub frame: pitch, velocity, gain into the effects, voice faders, master envelope, glide; or the Sub oscillator.
  11. The Mod shortcuts: drag one onto any control to modulate it.
  12. The band switch: M macros, K keyboard, C chord progression.
  13. The macros M1-M8 (or the keyboard, or the chord steps).
  14. The output column: Master volume, Limiter, meter, CPU, Rec.

The window can be resized by dragging its corner (from 50 to 300 %), or with Menu → Window Size (75, 100, 125, 150 or 200 %). Menu → Theme changes the colour: Cyan, Emerald, Azure, Violet or Silver. Both are remembered on this computer, not in presets.

In the standalone application, Menu → Standalone Tempo sets the tempo of everything synced to it (LFOs, Delay, Taps, envelopes with SYNC) when no host gives one: 20 to 300 BPM, remembered on this computer. In a host, the host's tempo leads.

6. The eight voices

Each voice plays the note you hold, multiplied by its ratio. The eight voices together make the chord. A voice with the Empty engine is silent and costs nothing.

The level of each voice is its fader in the Global frame, with marks at 0 and -6 dB.

Levels: you manage the gains

In Comma, 0 dB means "the level does not change". As on a mixing desk, nothing lowers the level behind your back: two voices at the same level are louder than one, muting a voice does not raise the others, and eight voices at full level are yours to bring down. A protection at the very end of the output only rounds off what would clip; it is not a level control.

The headroom is therefore visible, in the settings. A new sound starts with each voice's fader at -6 dB, Gain (level into the effects) at -3 dB and Master at -9 dB. A double-click brings these three back to those values. Presets keep their own levels.

Ratio presets

The list at the top right, (Ratio presets), stores ratios only: a chord for the eight voices. Loading one changes the notes of the chord, never the sound. The list also contains built-in chords. Its gold die rolls a chord in just ratios (octaves, fifths, thirds, natural harmonics); its pink die rolls ratios anywhere in the range ("mostly unusable, now and then something nobody would have written"). Right-click a die to undo.

7. The engines

The Engine page shows each voice's engine box. Every engine has a Vol (its level in the voice) and a KeyT (key tracking: at 100 the engine follows the note; at 0 it stays near C4 whatever you play, a fixed pitch or texture; in between it follows less). Several pitch controls have a Hz mode: click the label (Semi, Pitch or Cut) to set a fixed frequency instead, click it again to go back. In Hz mode the mouse wheel gives fine steps.

Click the title of an engine (ADD, IMPACT...) to open the list of engines and change it: the same list as in the voice head. Nothing changes until you choose; a second click closes the list. The engine of a muted voice turns pale.

Browsing. The graphs of Wave, Shard's tables, Impact, Sample and Noise have two arrows, left and right: they step through the list without opening it. The lists are ranked in families (the sub-folders of the library, for tables and samples).

Your own recipes. The lists of Impact, Sample and Noise store their settings as recipes. Change a recipe and the list shows Custom; in the right column of the list, Save, Save As, Rename and Delete manage yours (those of Comma are protected), and Recipes opens their folder. Yours appear in the same families. The dice draw only from those of Comma.

Missing files. When a preset asks for a table or a sample you do not have, the list or the graph says MISSING; hover it to read the name of the file. A missing table plays Basic.

Add

The additive engine: the voice is built from partials (harmonics), each computed on its own. It is the engine that the voice pages act on most finely, partial by partial.

Empty

No engine: the voice is silent and computes nothing. Its pages show "Empty slot: no engine."

8. The voice pages

The row under the main tabs chooses what every voice body shows. The pages follow the signal of the voice, left to right: engine (with Attack and PM) → Diss → Warp → Mangle → Pluck, Motion, Spectron, Capture → Env → Punch. Most pages are per voice; Motion and Capture are shared by all voices (the same settings appear in each row). PM exists for Add, Wave, Logic and Shard; a page that does not apply to an engine says "Not available for this engine, for now."

Punch

The last stage of each voice, after its envelope and its Attack layer: a compressor, then two controls that raise or tame the hit of each note and what rings after it. Attack and Sustain have no threshold: they follow the shape of the sound, not its level, so they act the same on a quiet or a loud voice.

ControlWhat it does
AttackThe hit, ±12 dB: a fast envelope against a slow one. Negative tames a percussion that pokes out of the mix; positive gives a soft sound more punch.
SustainWhat rings after the hit, ±12 dB, while the sound decays: negative tightens, positive lengthens the tail.
CompCompresses this voice before Attack and Sustain: one knob lowers the threshold and raises the ratio. It only lowers what goes over: bring the level back with Gain. 0 = off.

13. Modulation

The fastest way: drag and drop

Drag any Mod button (the rail on the left of the Modulation tab, or the Mod shortcuts at the bottom right of the other tabs), a macro label (M1-M8) or an XY label onto a control. While you drag, hovering a tab or a page button opens it; the control under the cursor lights up. On release a link is made at 100 %.

A modulated control shows a white arc (the reach of its links) and a coloured dot per source. To change the amount, right-click the control: Amount ±10 %, Invert sign, Set amount..., Remove link. Hover a Mod button, macro or XY label to see everything it moves.

The 32 sources

Click a Mod on the rail to show it; the small box at the start of its button shows the icon of its type and turns it on or off. Right-click: copy / paste a source, or copy it to all the others. The type selector, with the same icons, chooses the type; the page then shows only the settings of that type.

TypeWhat it is
CurveA shape you draw, looped. Click to add a point, drag to move, drag the ring to bend, double-click to remove; Grid X and Grid Y (also on right-click) say where the points snap. Grid X: the beats of the cycle in Sync (1/8, 1/16 by default, 1/32, 1/64: finer steps on a short cycle), Off, or 2 to 32 equal steps; Grid Y: Off, or 2 to 32 levels. A bank of curves with gold and pink dice.
RandomA new value each cycle (Smooth turns it into a glide).
ChaosA chaotic system as an LFO: System (Rossler, Lorenz, Duffing, Logistic) and Chaos (nearly periodic to fully chaotic).
StepA sequencer of 1 to 16 steps (click or drag on the graph). Quant (Free, 12, 24 levels), the number of steps (they always fill the whole cycle), Join (Hold, Lin, Curve), the reading order (Forward, Backward, Ping-pong: there and back, Random), Init, and dice (gold: a playable phrase; pink: everything).
EnvAn envelope started by each note, drawn on its graph (Smooth: the curve of its segments).
FollowFollows the level of the Sidechain input (0 in silence, 1 at full scale). Gain on the input before it is measured (-24 to +24 dB), and Band, whose two edges are a high-pass and a low-pass on what is measured only (for example: only the kick, only the hats). Attack and Release on its graph; Smooth: the curve of its value.

Common settings: Rate (0.02 to 20 Hz) or Div (note values of the host tempo): click the label to switch between them, each keeps its value. Smooth, Retrig (restart at each note), Uni (from 0 up instead of around 0), Trigger. An Env has no cycle: its Uni, Rate and Div are pale. A Follow is not started by anything: its Retrig and Trigger are pale too. Rate, Smooth and Div can themselves be modulated (a macro, another Mod): a modulated Div steps through the note values from the slowest to the fastest, so a wobble only ever speeds up.

Trigger says what restarts this source, besides Retrig. Note: only the note. Mod 1 to Mod 32: each step of that Mod (each cycle for a Curve, Random or Chaos; a Mod that is off, an Env or a Follow restarts nothing). A Step, Curve, Random or Chaos then starts its pattern again. An Env starts again while the note is held, and the note still starts it: a filter pulse on every step of a Step sequence, for example. A source restarted on each step plays only the part of its pattern that fits in one step, so give it a speed to match. A Mod aimed at itself does nothing, and its box goes pale.

The matrix

32 links, in two pages (1-16, 17-32). Each link: a source, a destination and an amount (-100 to +100 %; negative = the other way). The O / I switch at the top turns every link on or off; the sources keep running.

Sources: Mod 1-32, Macro 1-8, Vel (velocity of each note), Vel Hold (frozen at note-on), Mod Wheel, XY X, XY Y.

Destinations are grouped by section in the destination menu (Voices, Global, each engine, each page, the Mangle, each filter and effect, the mods themselves, the matrix amounts...). Per-voice destinations exist for V1 to V8. A destination generally uses the word you read on the control, written out, after its section and voice (Pluck Decay V1); the automation lists of your host use the same words.

When the source is a macro, a button beside the amount makes it bipolar (centre = zero, pushing both ways). This belongs to the macro: all its links follow.

Macros

Eight knobs, M1-M8. Drag a macro's label onto controls to link it. A macro takes the name of its first destination; double-click the label to rename it (a | forces a line break). Right-click the knob: Bipolar, or remove all its links. Double-click the knob: back to rest.

XY pad

On the Modulation tab. Drag the dot: its two axes are sources. The centre is rest (nothing is modulated there); double-click to recentre. The X and Y labels can be dragged onto controls, take the name of what they control, can be renamed by double-click, and a source dropped on a label modulates that axis.

Mod shortcuts

At the bottom right of the Synthesis, EQ's/Filters and FX tabs: Mod 1-16 or 17-32 (the two buttons on the left). Drag one onto a control. A shortcut that is in use lights up; hover it to read its links.

14. Playing: keyboard, chords, scale

K -- the keyboard

Click K at the bottom left. The on-screen keyboard plays Comma (your computer keyboard too, while this band is shown). The pitch wheel springs back to the centre; the mod wheel stays where you leave it (it follows CC1 of your MIDI keyboard; route it with the source Mod Wheel). The two small boxes set the bend range up and down (in semitones).

The keyboard opens on C1 and slides from C0 to C8 (C2 = MIDI note 48, 130.81 Hz, as in Bitwig). Drag past its left or right edge to slide it, the further the faster; the mouse wheel slides it too. The arrows at both ends light up when there are more notes that way: click one to jump an octave. On the computer keyboard, A plays C2, W C#2, S D2... up to K, C3. Vel 78: while lit, every key of the on-screen keyboard plays at 78.7 % velocity (Bitwig's default), wherever you click; off, the higher you click on a key, the softer it plays. A MIDI controller keeps its own velocity.

The upright Hold button holds the note: while it is lit, key releases (from the on-screen keyboard or from MIDI) wait, and apply when it goes off; a key still down keeps sounding. The sustain pedal (CC64) does the same while it is pressed, and lights Hold. Hold stays on when you switch to M or C: the notes keep sounding, and K stays lit.

All Sound Off (CC120) silences Comma at once, releases and effect tails included (in 10 ms, without a click). All Notes Off (CC123) releases the notes and lets the tails ring.

C -- the chord progression

Eight steps. Click a step's number to switch it on or off, its degree to choose I to VII of the scale, and below it to choose the chord (Major, Minor, Dim, Aug, Sus2, Sus4, 6, m6, 7, Maj7, m7, m7b5, dim7, 9, m9, Maj9, add9, or a ratio preset). With Prog on, each note you play takes the next active step: moved by its degree, played with its chord. Saved with the preset.

Scale

Scale at the top: choose a root (C to B) and a scale (Off, Major, Minor, Harmonic Minor, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Locrian, Pentatonic Major, Pentatonic Minor, Blues). Every note you play moves to the nearest note of the scale (a tie goes down). Saved with the preset.

Rec -- recording the output

Rec, in the output column, opens the recorder: a small panel that stays open from page to page. It turns the whole output of Comma into a sample, in one of two ways:

  • Last seconds: what you just played. Keep last seconds saves them; Length (1 to 29 s) says how far back it goes.
  • Next note: Arm, then play. The recording starts on your next note and stops when the sound has died out (effect tails included), or at Length. Click the same button again to cancel, or to stop a recording.

The Rec button is amber when armed, red while recording. The sample is trimmed, faded and set to -1 dB, then you give it a name and choose its folder in Samples (Captures, Kick, Snare, Hat, Clap, Perc, Texture, or a new one). Folder opens the Samples folder. Each of these folders is a family in the menus of the Sample engine and the Attack layer.

15. Presets and banks

The preset list (top bar)

Choose a preset to load it; the arrows go to the previous / next one. Its menu also holds Save, Save As, Reload, Rename, Delete and Init (Init loads DeFaUlT, the sound Comma starts on). A * before the name means the sound was changed since it was loaded.

Save As starts from the loaded preset, to save versions of it: its name with one version more (Strong Enough becomes Strong Enough v2, then v3...) and its Category, Type and Color; the note starts empty. From DeFaUlT, nothing is carried over.

The list shows the presets of the folder chosen in the Presets tab, with its sub-folders (All presets: the whole bank); the arrows step through that list. A preset loaded from elsewhere keeps its name in the box.

This menu has three columns, like the menus of the other banks and of the lists sorted in families (ratio presets, EQ and filter presets, wavetables, the Impact, Noise, Sample and Attack lists). On the left, the families, taken from the sub-folders: a click changes the middle column and leaves the menu open. In the middle, the contents of that family, with the current item highlighted. On the right, the actions (Save, Save As, Reload, Rename, Delete, Init on a bank; Open folder on the wavetables; Samples and Recipes on the lists that use them). You can make and delete your own folders there; Comma's own folders cannot be deleted. EQ and filter presets come in two families: Basic, simple shapes, and Presets, your own.

The Presets tab

  • Folders: click one to show its presets (and its sub-folders'). + makes a new folder; right-click a folder to rename or delete it (it goes to the Recycle Bin). Drag a preset from the list onto a folder to move it.
  • Search: keeps the presets whose name, author, tags, note or folder contain every word typed. At its right end, the count: how many presets the list shows, out of the bank.
  • Category, Type and Color: tag filters (the kind of sound, what it is within that kind, the feel of the sound: Lead, Bell, Bright). They add up. Each shows how many presets carry it (All: the whole bank). The Filter list is the same filter as Category.
  • The list: Name, Date (the last change of the file), Author, Category, Type, Color, Fav (0 to 5 stars), Note. Click a column to sort; drag a column header to move the column (the order is remembered).
  • The sheet on the right: Folder (Move...), Author and Note (written when you press Enter or leave the field), Category, Type and Color Tags... (tick tags, or type a new one; right-click a tag to rename it, or remove it, in every preset).
  • Load, Save (into the loaded preset), Save As, Rename, Delete (to the Recycle Bin).

16. The dice

Dice appear all over Comma. The convention is always the same:

  • Gold die: a playable roll -- tamed values, or another item of the same bank or category.
  • Pink die: anywhere -- the whole range, for happy accidents.
  • Silver die (voice head): the engine only.
  • Right-click any die: back to the state before the roll.

You will find them on the preset list, the ratio presets, each voice head, the Add, Chaos and Logic engines, the Impact, Noise, Sample and Attack lists, Spectron, each Warp slot, each Mangle block, every EQ and filter bank, the Sidechain shapes, the mod curves and the Step sequencer.

Mutate is the dice's gentle cousin: instead of rolling a new sound, it moves every continuous setting of the patch a little around its current value. The box beside it sets how far: Subtle, Medium or Wild. Below them, an orientation: Electrize (cyber, techno, new wave) mutates like Medium, then pushes the sound in that direction. Lists, switches, engines, routings, ratios, pitches, output levels and the two velocity settings never move; the master envelope moves in Wild only. And a setting left at zero or at either end of its course stays there -- what you switched off stays off. Click again to drift further; right-click: back to the values before the last mutation. The change happens during a short silence (about 75 ms), so a held note does not jump.

Signal flow

Each voice: engine (+ Attack Pre) -> Diss -> Warp 1 -> Warp 2 -> Mangle (Stutter, Shaper, Rate, Mod, Disperse) -> Pluck / Motion / Spectron / Capture -> [modules routed to this voice] -> Env x Vol -> pan (+ Attack Post) -> Punch (Comp, then Attack and Sustain) Groups: sum of the voices of A, B, C -> [modules routed to that group] Voices: sum of V1-V8 -> [modules routed to Voices] Mix: voices + Sub -> master envelope (Global) After: Crush -> Spatial (Width, M/S) -> [modules routed to All] -> FX rail in your order -> Sidechain (if last) -> Dynamics -> Master -> Limiter -> output

On an Add voice, Pluck, Motion, Spectron and Capture act on each partial, during the synthesis: there, Warp and Mangle come after them.

The modules are the ten of the EQ's/Filters tab (EQ A-D, F1-F4, Warmth, Modal). Each one goes where its Route says, and they always run in the order of their rail: drag a rail button to change it. With Warmth routed to All, Dist at the top of the FX rail runs before Warmth; lower on the rail, after it.

Tips

  • Comma is monophonic: to play chords, set the ratios (or use the ratio presets, or the chord progression).
  • An Empty voice costs nothing, and a stage that is switched off computes nothing. Watch the CPU readout.
  • Sample rate: 44.1 or 48 kHz is recommended, 96 kHz is fine. The load follows the rate: at 192 kHz the heaviest presets can use a whole core.
  • Set levels with the meter in LUFS mode (the white line is -17), and compare the effects with FX Off.
